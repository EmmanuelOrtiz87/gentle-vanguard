## Leverage Points (Meadows' Hierarchy)

*Least to most effective — higher numbers = higher leverage.*

| # | Leverage | Example |
|---|----------|---------|
| 12 | Parameters (constants, subsidies, taxes) | Price, tax rate, min wage |
| 11 | Buffer sizes (stabilizing stocks) | Inventory levels, cash reserves |
| 10 | Stock-and-flow structures | Pipeline stages, queue design |
| 9 | Delays (relative to change rates) | Shipping time, feedback cadence |
| 8 | Balancing loops (corrective feedback) | QA process, review gates |
| 7 | Reinforcing loops (growth engines) | Referral programs, network effects |
| 6 | Information flows (who knows what) | Dashboards, open-data policies |
| 5 | Rules (incentives, constraints, punishments) | Pricing models, governance |
| 4 | Self-organization (adding/evolving structure) | Team autonomy, hackathons |
| 3 | Goals (purpose of the system) | Mission, OKRs, success definition |
| 2 | Paradigms (mental models, assumptions) | "Growth at all costs" vs "sustainable" |
| 1 | Transcending paradigms | Seeing multiple models at once |

### Key Insight
Most people intervene at levels 10-12 (easy, visible, low leverage). The highest leverage is changing goals and paradigms (hard, invisible, lasting).
